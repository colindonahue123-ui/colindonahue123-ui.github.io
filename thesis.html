Online Nicotine Sales Restrictions on Adolescent Tobacco Use: Evidence from Arkansas' 2004 Ban

Colin Donahue Advanced Econometrics May 2022
Abstract

This paper studies the effects of Arkansas's 2005 ban on direct-to-consumer online tobacco sales on youth nicotine consumption. Using data from the Youth Risk Behavior Surveillance System (YRBS) between 2001 and 2007, difference-in-differences (DD) models are estimated across three control groups to identify the causal effect of the policy. Arkansas was selected as the treatment state due to the absence of significant concurrent tobacco legislation during the study period. Results suggest the ban was associated with a 6.67 percentage point reduction in the share of high school youth who smoke any cigarettes, and a 3.41 percentage point decrease in daily youth smokers, relative to all other states. Adult consumers were not significantly affected, suggesting the policy reduced youth access without substantially burdening legal-age consumers. Findings are relevant to ongoing federal debates about whether to extend online nicotine sale restrictions to e-cigarettes.
